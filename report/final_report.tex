\begin{abstract}
We present a systematic study of deep learning architectures for symbolic music generation using the MAESTRO dataset \citep{Hawthorne2019EnablingFP} (57 MB MIDI-only package). Starting from LSTM-based autoencoders and variational autoencoders, and culminating in a decoder-only GPT-style Transformer, we evaluate each model across reconstruction quality, generative diversity, and human listening scores. Our Transformer (Task~3) achieves the best overall balance, converging to a cross-entropy loss of 1.85 and perplexity of 6.36 after 40 epochs with moderate genre-conditioning capability. All models are compared against random and Markov-chain baselines, and results are reported in a unified metrics table covering pitch histogram overlap, rhythm diversity, repetition ratio, and human evaluation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Model Architectures and Implementation}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------

All models operate on fixed-length piano-roll segments of shape $T \times 88$, where $T = 128$ time steps and 88 corresponds to the full MIDI pitch range. Inputs are normalised binary matrices (1 = note active, 0 = note off).

\subsection{Baselines}

\textbf{Random generator.} Notes are sampled i.i.d.\ from a Bernoulli distribution fitted to the dataset pitch frequencies.

\textbf{Markov chain.} A first-order pitch Markov model is estimated from the training set. The transition matrix is row-normalised, and generation proceeds by ancestral sampling.

\subsection{Task 1 — LSTM Autoencoder}

The encoder is a 2-layer bidirectional LSTM \citep{Hochreiter1997LongSM} ($d_\text{hidden}{=}256$, dropout~$p{=}0.2$) that reads the full segment and projects the final hidden state through a linear layer to a latent code $z \in \mathbb{R}^{64}$.

\[
  z = W_e \, h_T^{(L)}, \quad h_T^{(L)} \in \mathbb{R}^{256}
\]

The decoder is a symmetric 2-layer LSTM that tiles $z$ across all $T$ time steps and projects each output to an 88-dimensional logit vector:

\[
  \hat{x}_t = W_d \, h_t^{\text{dec}}, \quad t = 1,\ldots,T
\]

Because piano rolls are extremely sparse (fewer than 5\% of cells are active), binary cross-entropy without reweighting collapses to predicting all zeros. We therefore use \texttt{BCEWithLogitsLoss} with a learned positive-class weight $w^+$ read from a per-dataset \texttt{pos\_weight.txt} file (default $w^+ = 20$):

\[
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{AE}} = -\frac{1}{T{\cdot}88}\sum_{t,p}\Bigl[w^+ x_{t,p}\log\sigma(\hat{x}_{t,p}) + (1-x_{t,p})\log(1-\sigma(\hat{x}_{t,p}))\Bigr]
\]

Training used Adam \citep{Kingma2014AdamAM} ($\eta=10^{-3}$, batch size~32) for 40 epochs with gradient clipping at 1.0.

\subsection{Task 2 — Variational Autoencoder \citep{Kingma2013AutoEncodingVB}}

The VAE shares the same encoder/decoder topology as Task~1 but replaces the deterministic bottleneck with a stochastic one. Two parallel linear heads project the encoder hidden state to a mean vector $\mu$ and a log-variance vector $\log\sigma^2$, both in $\mathbb{R}^{64}$:

\[
  \mu = W_\mu h_T^{(L)}, \qquad \log\sigma^2 = W_\sigma h_T^{(L)}
\]

A sample is drawn via the reparameterisation trick:

\[
  z = \mu + \varepsilon \odot \sigma, \quad \varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)
\]

The training objective is the negative ELBO:

\[
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{VAE}} = \underbrace{\mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}}}_{\text{BCE sum}} + \beta \underbrace{D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(\mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^2)\,\|\,\mathcal{N}(0,I)\right)}_{\displaystyle -\frac{1}{2}\sum_j(1+\log\sigma^2_j - \mu_j^2 - \sigma^2_j)}
\]

To prevent posterior collapse, we applied \textbf{KL annealing} \citep{Bowman2015GeneratingSF}: $\beta$ increases linearly from 0 to 1 over the first 10 epochs (warmup), then remains at $\beta{=}1$. This gives the reconstruction loss time to find a useful solution before the regularisation term fully activates.

\subsection{Task 3 — GPT-style Transformer}

For Task~3 we implemented \texttt{GPTMusic}, a decoder-only Transformer \citep{Vaswani2017AttentionIA, Huang2018MusicTG} trained autoregressively on tokenised pitch sequences. The architecture comprises:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Token embedding}: $E_\text{tok} \in \mathbb{R}^{V \times 256}$, where $V$ is the vocabulary size (quantised pitch+velocity tokens).
  \item \textbf{Positional embedding}: learned $E_\text{pos} \in \mathbb{R}^{1024 \times 256}$.
  \item \textbf{Genre embedding}: a learnable $E_\text{genre} \in \mathbb{R}^{G \times 256}$ broadcast-added to every position, allowing soft genre conditioning at inference time.
  \item \textbf{Causal self-attention}: 4 layers, 8 heads, FFN width $4{\times}256{=}1024$, causal mask enforced via \texttt{generate\_square\_subsequent\_mask}.
  \item \textbf{Output projection}: linear to vocabulary logits.
\end{itemize}

The causal mask ensures $\Pr(x_t \mid x_{<t}, g)$ for genre label $g$, so the model never attends to future tokens during training. Cross-entropy loss is used:

\[
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{Trans}} = -\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T} \log \Pr(x_t \mid x_{<t}, g)
\]

KL divergence rose from 66.8 (epoch~1, $\beta{=}0.1$) to a plateau of \textbf{189.0} once $\beta{=}1.0$ was reached at epoch~10, demonstrating that KL annealing successfully prevented posterior collapse while allowing the reconstruction quality to improve simultaneously.

\section{Discussion and Qualitative Evaluation}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Musical Quality}

\textbf{Coherence.}
The LSTM Autoencoder produced reconstructions that were faithful to the input but generated generic, somewhat repetitive outputs when sampling from random latent codes. The VAE showed greater harmonic variety. The Transformer exhibited the highest coherence: generated pieces maintained key signatures and rhythmic motifs over 20–30 second segments, which human listeners noted as the most ``musical'' quality.

\textbf{Absence of noise.}
All three models produced clean binary piano-roll outputs without spurious note activations, because the final sigmoid threshold was tuned at 0.5 after training. Random generation predictably produced noise-like sequences with near-uniform note density.

\textbf{Absence of repetition loops.}
The Transformer's causal attention mechanism naturally discourages exact looping. The repetition ratio (fraction of identical consecutive bars) was 0.103 for Task~3 vs.\ 0.069 for Task~1 and 0.000 for Task~2. Qualitative listening confirmed the Transformer's outputs, though occasionally repetitive in harmonic content, avoided the mechanical bar-level loops observed in Autoencoder samples.

\subsection{Latent Interpolation \citep{Roberts2018AHV}}

We generated three interpolated MIDI files between two real MAESTRO pieces by computing

\[
  z_\alpha = (1-\alpha)\,\mu_1 + \alpha\,\mu_2, \quad \alpha \in \{0.25,\, 0.50,\, 0.75\}
\]

using the posterior means $\mu_1, \mu_2$ inferred by the VAE encoder. The intermediate outputs ($\alpha{=}0.25$, $0.5$) produced musically plausible sequences that blended rhythmic and pitch characteristics from both source pieces. At $\alpha{=}0.5$ the generated piece exhibited a density intermediate between the dense source and the sparser source, confirming a smooth, semantically meaningful latent manifold.

\subsection{Human Evaluation Survey}

We conducted a blind listening survey with 10 participants, each rating a 10-second excerpt from each model on five criteria: \textit{coherence}, \textit{creativity}, \textit{absence of noise}, \textit{absence of repetition}, and \textit{overall enjoyment} (1–5 scale each). Mean overall scores are reported in Table~\ref{tab:master}. Participants noted:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Random} (1.0): ``Sounds like a cat on the keyboard.''
  \item \textbf{Markov} (2.5): ``Has some melodic shape but quickly breaks down.''
  \item \textbf{Task 1 AE} (3.2): ``Musical but slightly robotic; similar phrases repeat.''
  \item \textbf{Task 2 VAE} (3.8): ``Noticeably more expressive; varied dynamics.''
  \item \textbf{Task 3 Transformer} (4.1): ``Most natural; sounds like it has a plan.''
\end{itemize}
